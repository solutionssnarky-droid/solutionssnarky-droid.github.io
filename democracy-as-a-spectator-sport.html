Democracy as a Spectator Sport

Everyone owns a jersey. Nobody has read the job description.

Snarky Solutions, 28 October 2026

The chain: we cheer, so nobody checks. So candidates win on loyalty. So institutions go unaudited. So the bill lands on jobs and business. And the checkers are understaffed. So people take to the streets. So the job lands on you.

We cheer, so nobody checks

Pick a jersey, learn the chants, defend your team’s no-ball like it’s a Fundamental Right. Researchers call it affective polarization. Dias and Lelkes (AJPS, 2022) find that identity, more than policy, drives hostility between sides. In the US, the rival-party “feeling thermometer” fell from 41.6 in 2000 to 19.3 in 2020 (ANES). Some researchers say that partly measures dislike of party elites, not ordinary people.

The upshot: a fan asks which side a fact helps. An auditor asks if it’s true. Democracy needs the second kind, and this read tries to be one.

So candidates win on loyalty, not record

In Lok Sabha 2024, 251 of 543 winners (46%) declared criminal cases in their own affidavits, up from 30% in 2009 (ADR). Candidates with declared cases had a 15.3% chance of winning; clean ones, 4.4%. Across parties: 94 of the BJP’s 240 winners, 13 of the DMK’s 22. Declared is not convicted (27 were), and the data is self-declared. But when loyalty is guaranteed, “winnability” beats “clean record”.

Your job: read the affidavit on MyNeta before the manifesto.

So the institutions underneath go unaudited

Exams. NEET-UG 2026 was cancelled on 12 May over a paper leak, hitting about 22 lakh students; re-test on 21 June. A Tribune tally counts 90+ leaks and irregularities in two decades.

Food. In 2025-26, 40,023 of 2,23,808 samples (about 18%) were non-conforming, against 1,918 convictions (Lok Sabha reply). The two numbers come from different pipelines, but the gap is the story. The response included a hashtag. Your paneer remains unimpressed.

So the bill lands on jobs and business

Jobs. Headline unemployment was 5.5% in May 2026 (PLFS). In 2023-24, graduate unemployment was 10.6% for men and 20.4% for women (Rajya Sabha reply). The measure moves the number: youth estimates run from roughly 10% to 15%, so whoever picks the measure wins the argument.

Business. About ₹8.1 lakh crore is stuck in delayed MSME payments (Economic Survey 2025-26). The law says 45 days; Recordent puts the average invoice cycle at 73. Many small firms don’t sue, because they’d like to keep the customer who isn’t paying.

And the checkers are understaffed

Exams, food and payments are supposed to have watchdogs. The slow-burn ones are thin:

  • Local government. A 2024 CAG audit found polls delayed in about 60% of urban local governments. In Maharashtra, some corporations’ terms had expired as early as 2020 before the Supreme Court set a January 2026 deadline.
    Your job: know your councillor’s name and when the next civic poll is due.
  • Parliament. The 17th Lok Sabha averaged 55 sitting days a year; the first averaged 135 (PRS). In the 18th, only 11 of 42 bills went to committees (The Federal).
    Your job: read the PRS summary of any bill that touches you, and write to your MP.
  • Courts. 4.98 crore cases pending in district courts and 95,936 in the Supreme Court (28 July 2026, NJDG).
    Your job: try mediation or Lok Adalat, and know free legal aid exists via your District Legal Services Authority.

So people take to the streets

Two youth-led movements, one autumn: France’s lycée protests (since 18 Sept) and India’s Cockroach Janta Party-led protests over exams and the voter-roll revision (SIR).

In Bihar’s earlier revision, the Supreme Court ordered the ECI to publish the 65 lakh deleted names with reasons. About 13 crore names have reportedly been removed nationally, which the ECI frames as clean-up. Which side is right is for the courts and the evidence, not rally headcounts.

So the job lands on you

Article 51A lists citizens’ duties. We recite rights on demand and duties never. A practical version:

  1. Yearly: check your name on the electoral roll.
  2. Every election, even the boring ones: vote in the municipal and panchayat polls.
  3. Quarterly: read one bill summary or your MP’s record (PRS).
  4. Before every forward: which source, which year, which definition?
  5. Always: protest peacefully, defend that right for people you disagree with, and hold your own side to the standard you apply to the other.

A team can win and the country can still lose. Only one of those gets a scoreboard.
